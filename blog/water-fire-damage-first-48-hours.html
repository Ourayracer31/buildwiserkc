Water & Fire Damage in Multi-Family Buildings: The First 48 Hours

Build Wiser Construction · Kansas City, MO · Updated August 2026

After water or fire damage in a multi-family building, speed decides the cost. Stop the source, make sure residents are safe, document everything for insurance, and get a restoration contractor on site fast — mold can begin growing in wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, turning a cleanup into a full remediation.

Why the first 48 hours matter

Damage doesn't stay put. Water wicks into drywall, subfloors and shared wall cavities, spreading to neighboring units. Smoke residue becomes more corrosive and harder to clean the longer it sits. The faster mitigation starts, the smaller the eventual scope — which is why 24/7 response exists in this industry.

Water damage: what to do first

  • Stop the source — shut off the water supply or the affected riser to stop more water entering.
  • Protect people first — watch for slip hazards and, near any outlet or panel, electrical risk; cut power to wet areas if it's safe to do so.
  • Call for extraction — get a restoration crew started on water removal and drying; standing water and saturated materials only get worse with time.
  • Move what you can — lift or remove contents from wet areas to limit damage and help drying.

Fire and smoke damage: what to do first

  • Wait for clearance — no one re-enters until the fire department says the structure is safe.
  • Secure the building — board openings and tarp the roof so weather and intruders don't add damage.
  • Limit smoke spread — soot and odor migrate through HVAC and hallways; a restoration team can contain and start cleaning affected areas quickly.
  • Don't wipe soot yourself — improper cleaning can set stains permanently; leave it to restoration crews with the right methods.

Documenting for insurance

Before cleanup changes the scene, photograph and video everything — the source, the spread, damaged contents and affected rooms. Keep a simple log of what happened and when. Good documentation speeds the claim and protects you from disputes later. A restoration contractor experienced with insurance work will document scope in the same language your adjuster uses.

What a restoration contractor does

A full-service restoration team handles the whole arc: emergency mitigation (extraction, drying, containment, board-up), then teardown of unsalvageable materials, mold prevention and remediation where needed, and finally the rebuild — drywall, paint, flooring, cabinets and fixtures — back to finished, rent-ready condition. Using one contractor for mitigation and rebuild keeps the timeline tight and the scope consistent.
