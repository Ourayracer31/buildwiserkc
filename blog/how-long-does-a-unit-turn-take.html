Unit Turns

How Long Does a Multi-Family Unit Turn Take?

Build Wiser Construction · Kansas City, MO · Updated August 2026

Most multi-family unit turns take 3 to 10 business days. A standard cosmetic turn — paint, cleaning, minor repairs and flooring — runs 3 to 5 business days, while a full turn with cabinets, countertops and appliances typically takes 1 to 2 weeks. Damage repair or a gut renovation can push a single unit to three weeks or more.

What is a unit turn?

A unit turn is the work that gets a unit from move-out to rent-ready for the next resident. Depending on the condition, that can mean anything from a quick paint-and-clean to replacing flooring, cabinets, counters, appliances and fixtures. The faster a turn is done well, the sooner the unit is listed and earning again — so timeline and quality both matter.

Typical unit-turn timelines in Kansas City

Timelines vary with scope, unit size and how much damage is present. As a general guide for KC-metro multi-family units:

What slows a unit turn down?

  • Hidden damage — water, mold or subfloor issues found after tear-out add scope and time.
  • Material lead times — special-order cabinets, counters or appliances can add days or weeks.
  • Trade sequencing — when plumbing, electrical, drywall and flooring aren't scheduled tightly, units sit idle between steps.
  • Access and approvals — slow sign-offs on scope or color selections stall the crew.

How do you speed up a unit turn?

The fastest turns come from planning, not rushing. We keep timelines tight by:

  • Walking the unit early and locking scope and selections before demo.
  • Ordering long-lead materials up front so they arrive before they're needed.
  • Sequencing trades back-to-back so the unit is never waiting on the next crew.
  • Assigning a single point of contact who keeps the owner updated and decisions moving.

Turning several units at once? We sequence crews across units so trades stay productive and your community relists blocks of inventory on a predictable schedule — not one slow unit at a time.

Turning multiple units at a time

For larger make-readies and value-add projects, the per-unit timeline matters less than throughput. By staggering demo, rough-in and finish crews across multiple units, a well-run turn program keeps every trade busy and delivers a steady flow of rent-ready units rather than finishing them one by one.
